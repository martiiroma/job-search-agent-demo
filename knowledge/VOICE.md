# VOICE.md

**Role:** how a cover letter is written, and what it may claim.
**Built from:** five real letters with a known outcome.
**Edited by:** the human only.

---

## 0. Rules of use

1. **No claim without a record.** Everything a letter says must trace back to `CV-MASTER.md`.
2. **Job titles are canonical.** Never translated, never swapped for a synonym.
3. **The letter is written in the language of the offer.**

---

## 1. Guiding principle — talk about the job, not the candidate

The letter that worked best was not the best written. One letter full of template phrases got an interview; a well-written one got no answer. What made the difference: **the successful letter opened by naming the organisation's real problem**, and only then mapped the candidate to it.

**Validation test:** if a paragraph could be pasted unchanged into a letter for another organisation in the same sector, it is about the candidate, not the job. Rewrite it.

---

## 2. Structure

**Four paragraphs · 250–300 words · max 1,900 characters** *(limits measured from the template, not chosen by eye).*

| # | Function |
|---|---|
| 1 | Anchor: an opening from §3, tied to this specific role |
| 2 | Main evidence for the offer's most important requirement |
| 3 | Secondary evidence and what the candidate adds |
| 4 | A close that names this organisation's mission. Never generic |

---

## 3. Openings — rotating catalogue

The agent records which opening it used last and **never uses the same one twice in a row**.

| | Opening | Note |
|---|---|---|
| A | The organisation's real problem | Preferred. Best documented result |
| B | A verifiable fact from the candidate's track record | Never a self-description |
| C | A specific line of the offer that resonates | — |
| D | The organisation's context and value | Works well in research and the third sector |
| E | A personal conviction about the craft | Max. once every five letters |

---

## 4. Never

- Abstract self-description: "passion for", "innate ability", "unique blend"
- Apologies or unrequested mentions of a gap
- Applying to two roles in one letter
- Describing a finished role in the present tense
- Requirements the candidate cannot meet. They are neither mentioned nor disguised
